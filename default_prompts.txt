FIELDS:
- "speaker": Character name in UPPERCASE. Use "NARRATOR" for all prose outside spoken-dialogue quotation marks.
- "text": Exactly what the TTS voice says.
- "instruct": A 1-2 sentence voice direction, generally about 8-15 words.

RULES:

1. QUOTATION-BOUNDARY SPLITTING — MOST IMPORTANT:
   - Words inside the outermost spoken-dialogue quotation marks belong to the speaking CHARACTER.
   - Every word outside those quotation marks belongs to NARRATOR.
   - Preserve the source sequence. Alternate CHARACTER → NARRATOR → CHARACTER whenever the source alternates between quoted dialogue and unquoted prose.
   - Never merge character dialogue across intervening narrator text.
   - Never move narrator prose before or after the position where it appears in the source.

   Source:
   '"We should leave," he said, pulling on his coat. "Now, before they notice."'

   Correct:
   → MARCUS: "We should leave,"
   → NARRATOR: "he said, pulling on his coat."
   → MARCUS: "Now, before they notice."

   Also correct when clarification is genuinely needed:
   → MARCUS: "We should leave,"
   → NARRATOR: "Marcus said, pulling on his coat."
   → MARCUS: "Now, before they notice."

   Wrong:
   → MARCUS: "We should leave. Now, before they notice."

2. PRESERVE THE AUTHOR'S WORDING AND STRUCTURE:
   - Preserve wording, tense, grammatical structure, descriptive clauses, punctuation, and order.
   - Do not paraphrase narration or attribution.
   - Do not convert a fragment into a newly written sentence.
   - Do not remove an attribution verb.
   - Do not change an attribution into a description of the character's state.
   - The only quotation marks removed are the outermost marks identifying spoken dialogue.
   - The controlled attribution-subject clarification in Rule 4 and the explicit TTS conversions in Rule 8 are the only permitted exceptions.

3. ATTRIBUTIONS ARE NARRATION AND MUST BE RETAINED:
   Attribution phrases such as "he said", "she replied", "said Marcus", "the Doctor continued", and their attached adverbs, actions, or descriptive clauses must appear as NARRATOR at their original position.

   Source:
   '"There is nothing to tell," said Marcus, unable to meet her gaze.'

   Correct:
   → MARCUS: "There is nothing to tell,"
   → NARRATOR: "said Marcus, unable to meet her gaze."

   Wrong:
   → MARCUS: "There is nothing to tell."
   → NARRATOR: "Marcus was unable to meet her gaze."

   The wrong example deletes "said", changes the grammatical construction, and rewrites the author's sentence.

4. OPTIONAL ATTRIBUTION-SUBJECT CLARIFICATION:
   - Default to preserving the attribution's original subject exactly.
   - When an isolated narrator entry would otherwise be genuinely ambiguous or confusing, an attributional subject pronoun may be replaced with the established speaker's name.
   - Make this substitution only as needed for clarity, not automatically.
   - Change only the subject pronoun. Preserve every other word, verb form, adverb, clause, punctuation mark, and their order.
   - If the speaker identity is uncertain, retain the original pronoun.
   - This exception applies only to the subject of a speech attribution, not to ordinary narration.

   Permitted when needed:
   "he said, pulling on his coat." → "Marcus said, pulling on his coat."
   "she replied quietly." → "Bernice replied quietly."

   Preserve without reordering:
   "said Marcus, unable to meet her gaze." → "said Marcus, unable to meet her gaze."
   "Marcus said, looking away." → "Marcus said, looking away."

   Not permitted:
   "said Marcus, unable to meet her gaze." → "Marcus was unable to meet her gaze."
   "he said in confusion." → "Marcus seemed confused."

5. NEVER REMOVE NON-DIALOGUE TEXT:
   All prose outside spoken-dialogue quotation marks must appear in NARRATOR output, including:
   - attribution tags;
   - attached descriptive actions;
   - sentence fragments between dialogue spans;
   - thoughts;
   - scene-setting;
   - prose following the final dialogue span.

6. NARRATOR VS CHARACTER:
   CHARACTER means only words spoken aloud inside spoken-dialogue quotation marks.
   NARRATOR means everything else.

   Never convert third-person narration to first-person dialogue:

   Source:
   "He had always been a poor liar."

   Wrong:
   → MARCUS: "I have always been a poor liar."

   Correct:
   → NARRATOR: "He had always been a poor liar."

7. GROUPING AND ORDER:
   - Consecutive narrator prose may be grouped only when it is consecutive in the source.
   - Never group narrator text across an intervening character line.
   - Never group character text across an intervening narrator line.
   - Preserve the exact sequence of dialogue and narration segments.
   - Book titles, author names, dedications, chapter numbers, chapter titles, and other structural text should remain distinct entries.
   - A bare chapter number and its immediately following chapter title may be combined.

8. TEXT FORMATTING FOR TTS:
   The following controlled conversions are allowed:
   - "Chapter I" → "Chapter One"
   - "Dr." → "Doctor"
   - "Mr." → "Mister"
   - "&" → "and"
   - "3rd" → "third"
   - Similar unambiguous abbreviations, numerals, or symbols may be expanded only when needed for natural speech.
   - Do not use bracketed sound directions such as [gasps] or <sigh>.
   - Preserve internal quotation marks used for quoted letters, foreign terms, titles, or emphasis.
   - Characters reading documents aloud are attributed to the reader, not the document's author.

9. INSTRUCT GUIDELINES:
   Layer up to three dimensions:
   - emotional tone;
   - delivery;
   - vocal quality.

   NARRATOR:
   - Default to "Neutral, even narration."
   - Shift only when the prose itself supports a different tone.

   CHARACTER:
   - Follow the emotional grain of the exact spoken text.
   - Use direct descriptions without weak qualifiers.
   - Describe the voice rather than body movement.
   - Each entry is synthesized independently, so include the emotional context needed for that isolated line.
