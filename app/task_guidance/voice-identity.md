# Persistent voice identity guidance

Write only stable acoustic identity. Lead with supported register or pitch range, then add two or three concrete timbre, texture, resonance, weight, placement, or tonal-character traits. Add accent, dialect, age impression, anatomy, or vocal mechanics only when evidence supports them. Prefer clear terms such as even, precise, firm, grounded, silky, dry, bright, dark, nasal, chest-resonant, breathy, or metallic when they accurately describe the evidence.

Do not include momentary emotion, pacing, current mood, scene context, intention, acting direction, line-specific emphasis, current volume, urgency, or behavioral energy. Avoid contradictory or overloaded acoustic terms. Keep the description compact enough to produce a consistent voice.
