# Complete Cast dossier guidance

Treat the selected source and Alexandria records as evidence, not as permission to invent biography, appearance, or acoustic facts.

## One coordinated identity system

Use the same identities across the roster, Voice, and visual sections. Preserve exact Script speaker labels for Voice dossiers. Use roster identity seeds, canonical names, display names, and aliases consistently in visual records. Do not create separate identities merely because a character appears under a title, nickname, age, disguise, narration role, or temporary state.

## Roster and relationships

Discover people, narrator roles, groups, creatures, named non-speakers, and other recurring entities. Record aliases, titles, roles, pronouns, species or type, speaking status, relationships, and sample lines only when supported by exact evidence. Search the whole source for relationship evidence rather than treating the first mention as complete. Keep ambiguous family, romantic, professional, adversarial, ownership, membership, and travelling relationships uncertain instead of resolving them by assumption.

## Voice persona and Designed Voice definition

For every supplied Script speaker, create:

- a concise performance persona describing stable character identity relevant to acting;
- a compact synthesis-ready Designed Voice description describing persistent acoustic identity;
- one exact representative Script line as ref_text;
- structured acoustic traits for vocal age impression, pitch, weight and resonance, texture and timbre, accent and language, cadence and rhythm, energy range, emotional range, and casting guidance.

Every acoustic trait must declare its basis:

- `explicit`: directly stated in the source;
- `inferred`: strongly supported by multiple supplied cues;
- `casting_recommendation`: a creative production recommendation rather than a source fact;
- `unknown`: not supportable from the supplied material.

Explicit and inferred traits require exact evidence quotes. Casting recommendations may fill production gaps, but must never be presented as canonical character facts. Do not put momentary emotion, current scene state, or one-line delivery direction into the persistent Voice definition.

## Visual dossiers

Extract exact visual observations before compiling dossiers. Distinguish stable physical traits from clothing, temporary condition, disguise, age-specific state, location, and scene variants. Preserve conflicts and unknowns. Do not infer race, disability, body shape, attractiveness, costume, color, anatomy, or cinematic styling without evidence. A visual dossier may remain sparse when the source is sparse.

## Completeness

When a section is selected, return the complete selected section rather than a sample. Voice dossiers must cover every supplied Script speaker exactly once. Visual work should account for every roster identity for which source-supported visual evidence exists and explicitly preserve unknowns where it does not.
